\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Autobahn provides the multi-lane dissemination and cut-based ordering foundation; Multimmit is our selected implementation base, not merely a candidate. We define a common ordered-prefix contract while retaining each protocol's native voting and serialization. Multimmit already discusses early execution for chain-partitioned applications. Our distinct target is shared-state speculation before global ordering is settled, safe reuse, and operation-aware whole-transaction placement; neither early execution in general nor state partitioning is claimed as new. Multimmit-only measurements do not establish Autobahn performance. \citep{autobahn,multimmit}

Block-STM and Aggregators V2 provide the backend selected for the benchmark, not the paper's central contribution. Their preset-order validation and delayed-value processing are prior work. The proposed pipeline must validate reuse across changing candidates regardless of the chosen backend; the benchmark adapter must demonstrate that it meets this requirement. Placement effects are evaluated separately from existing dependency-waiting behavior. \citep{blockstm,aip47}

Zaptos overlaps consensus with execution, certification, and storage, establishing overlap as prior work. Our focus is the multi-producer DA-to-execution pipeline and operation-aware placement within an Autobahn-family implementation, rather than a cross-system comparison with Zaptos. \citep{zaptos}

Shard Scheduler makes account-placement and migration decisions deterministic and verifiable as part of block validation. Its history-based affinity and load statistics balance locality against shard load. TxAllo uses historical transaction relationships and load-aware account allocation. Our proposal borrows reproducible placement, but assigns whole transactions to producers without migrating state ownership. The target is late-predecessor invalidation in a pre-cut execution pipeline, not cross-shard transaction cost. Neither work establishes that our proxy minimizes re-execution or improves future end-to-end latency; those claims require our own evaluation. \citep{shardscheduler,txallo}

Schism derives workload affinities from transaction accesses to guide balanced database partitioning. KaHyPar provides the multilevel coarsening and refinement precedent; deterministic parallel hypergraph partitioning shows how reproducible refinement can coexist with practical parallel search. Our adaptation retains original-scope plurality routes and evaluates each lifted move with a transaction-level dependency/load/reassignment cost, rather than treating a native hyperedge connectivity objective as equivalent. These works motivate the representation and search architecture, not our Byzantine update protocol or a theorem about state-finalization latency. \citep{schism,kahypar,deterministicHypergraph}

PBFT's matching replies and authenticated state transfer are established foundations; neither \(f+1\) nor applying fetched state is claimed as a standalone contribution. \citep{pbft}

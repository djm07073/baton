For an Ethereum-compatible backend, the reader can reuse the complete Reth pool/network/maintenance composition demonstrated by Tempo, its public dependency-aware best iterator/filter hooks and canonical update contract. Its Ethereum types/provider/fees/nonces and optional Tempo extensions are explicit constraints. Removing discarded entries, iterator invalidation, mined pruning and full canonical account/tip refresh have different meanings. Static payload classification/complete body packing attaches to selected material; imported/durable outcomes must map to the compatible backend, not to an invented account refresh engine.

For a Constantinople-compatible source adaptation, the reader can retain its bounded foreground/background owner, dedup and byte-aware whole-batch selection. Private ingestion must be exposed/adapted; selected batches are popped and proposal digests alone do not retain canceled bytes. The application must reconcile ownership/reselection and durable outcomes. The wrapper's submission receiver resolves batch status, not canonical execution certification. It is a publish=false source package and older graph, not a public drop-in generic mempool.
